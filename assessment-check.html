Practice: Google Maps

Scenario – Using Google Maps

A student is travelling from Hokitika to Greymouth for a sports event. They use Google Maps to find the venue, plan their journey and navigate to their destination.

After entering the destination, Google Maps displays possible routes, estimated travel times and distances. The student selects a route and starts navigation.

During the journey, Google Maps displays their current location, upcoming turns and estimated arrival time. The student can follow their progress on the map as they travel.

Partway through the journey, the student decides to take a different route. Google Maps allows them to select an alternative route or stop navigation if their plans change.

Consider: How do these features help the student complete their journey? How effectively does Google Maps support the needs of its users?

Interface: Google Maps

Purpose: To help users find locations, plan routes and navigate to destinations.

Usability Principle 1 – Visibility of system status

Statement: Google Maps effectively applies visibility of system status to help the student navigate to the sports venue.

Explain or Expand: This principle means keeping users informed about what the system is doing and their progress towards completing a task.

Example or Evidence: During navigation, Google Maps displays the student's current location, upcoming turns and estimated arrival time.

Discuss or Depth: These features help the student recognise whether they are following the correct route and when they need to turn. However, inaccurate GPS information could temporarily reduce the reliability of this feedback.

Usability Principle 2 – User control and freedom

Statement: Google Maps also effectively applies user control and freedom by allowing the student to change or stop navigation.

Explain or Expand: This principle means users should be able to change their decisions or leave a process without being trapped in an unwanted action.

Example or Evidence: For example, the student can select an alternative route to Greymouth or stop navigation if their plans change.

Discuss or Depth: This is effective because the student can adjust their journey without having to follow an unsuitable route. It supports Google Maps' purpose of helping users reach their destinations while allowing them to make their own travel decisions.
