Digital media

Teacher planning reference

Overview

Students investigate existing digital media and apply conventions, design principles and technical skills to create their own outcomes for a purpose, audience and platform. Year 9 also emphasises managing digital assets and files: tools, formats, naming, versions, exports, backups and evidence. Year 10 distinguishes interactive, information-based and time-based media and runs a manageable digital project from a brief through requirements, scope, trialling and audience feedback.

This builds on the Year 7–8 media and file-organisation expectations in Data and Information.

Official Curriculum Strand

Digital media is one of the four headings (Computer science, Programming, Digital media, Responsible practice) that organise the Year 9–10 Digital Technology teaching sequence. It is not a separate official strand. Source: pp. 5 and 24.

Design, Make, and Innovate underpins all Digital Technology outcomes. In both Year 9 and Year 10, each student must be taught Design, Make, and Innovate through at least two discipline-specific strands. Copyright, attribution, consent and AI-generated assets connect to Responsible practice; interactive media may also draw on Programming. Source: pp. 5, 19 and 24.

Read across each topic. Year 9 builds conventions and asset management; Year 10 adds media types and project management. Paraphrased summaries: p. 24.

Design, Make, and Innovate

Media outcomes should be designed for a real audience and refined from evidence. Paraphrased knowledge and practices: p. 19; integration: p. 24.

Learning Contexts

Future activities - proposed, not yet added

  • Media analysis: deconstruct an existing website, poster or video and record its conventions and design principles.
  • Asset pipeline: collect, name, version and export images and audio for a project, with backups.
  • Client media brief (Year 10): plan an interactive, information-based or time-based outcome with requirements, scope and timeline; a MiddleDTECH Jobs card can provide the client.
  • Audience test: trial a draft with real users, then record and justify changes.

Teacher Notes

Curriculum coverage

Direct links are conventions, design principles and asset/file management in Year 9, and media types and manageable project planning in Year 10 (p. 24).

Gaps to plan for

  • A finished product alone does not show investigation of existing outcomes or file-management practice.
  • Audience feedback and technical trialling need deliberate prompts in Year 10.
  • Sourced assets need attribution and permission (see Responsible practice).

Possible evidence - teacher judgement required

Collect analyses of existing media, design representations, organised project files, briefs, feedback and explained refinements.

No grades, progress descriptors or automatic curriculum-completion rules are assigned here. Check the eventual official curriculum before treating the mapping as a compliance reference.
