Teacher planning reference

Overview

Students investigate systems made from interacting mechanical, electronic and digital parts. They build, represent and test purposeful outcomes, considering feedback, control, movement, forces and accessibility. Year 8 extends this into annotated subsystem explanations, component selection, integration and refinement using feedback or performance data.

This page maps the school card to the official Systems and Control strand. It is a teacher reference, not a student task checklist or confirmation that a kit covers the whole strand.

Official Curriculum Strand

Systems and Control is the named discipline-specific strand, distinct from Digital Technology. Design, Make, and Innovate underpins its purposeful design and development. Source: pp. 5, 13 and 16.

Programmed components can be taught alongside Digital Technology, or Systems and Control can use pre-programmed components independently. A controlled circuit does not automatically show student programming knowledge. See Programming & Algorithms for a separate programming mapping. Source: pp. 16 and 17.

Year 7 and Year 8 Knowledge and Practices

Read across each topic for commonality, with knowledge and practices kept distinct. Paraphrased curriculum summaries: p. 16.

Systems and Control: aligned Year 7 and Year 8 expectations
Common topic and pathway fitYear 7 Knowledge and PracticesYear 8 Knowledge and Practices
Systems and subsystems
Systems thinking

Knowledge: Mechanical, electronic and digital systems contain smaller systems with specific functions that contribute to an overall purpose.

Practices: Deconstruct an everyday system to identify interactions among electrical, electronic, mechanical and structural parts.

Knowledge: System diagrams explain input/process/output, subsystems, feedback and control loops. Hardware, software and networks work together to process/share information.

Practices: Produce annotated diagrams explaining subsystems, reliability risks and input-to-output transformations.

Feedback and control
Systems thinking

Knowledge: Systems respond to inputs; a feedback loop compares output with a target and adjusts input to reduce the difference.

Practices: Build and test simple systems containing feedback, control and basic accessibility features.

Knowledge: Feedback and performance evidence help identify faults and improve reliability and usability.

Practices: Build/test circuits with manual or simple programmable control, and refine systems using user feedback or performance data.

Symbols and component choices
Representation and electronics

Knowledge: Standard symbols describe basic components and connections. Electrical outcomes use energy for straightforward functions; electronic circuits process/control information through inputs and outputs.

Practices: Represent functions and connections using standard symbols.

Knowledge: Component values, colour codes, voltage, current and resistance inform component choice and circuit behaviour.

Practices: Select components using data sheets and colour codes; document connections with appropriate conventions.

Movement, forces and structures
Physical systems

Knowledge: Movement can be linear, rotary, reciprocating or oscillating. Compression, tension, shear and torsion describe structural loads and resistance.

Practices: Compare how structures carry loads, move and resist forces; investigate how simple machines/mechanisms change movement or force.

Knowledge: Integrated systems combine mechanisms, structures, circuits, controls and simple programmable components to transfer energy, move, process signals and respond.

Practices: Integrate systems and document mechanisms, movement, forces, control methods and connections.

Purposeful outcomes and safe practice
Shared requirement

Knowledge/responsibility: Manage supported risks and follow relevant safety procedures for tools, equipment, electrical systems and powered parts.

Practices: Design and make an authentic Systems and Control outcome suited to its purpose.

Knowledge/responsibility: The same safety expectations apply throughout design, construction, testing and evaluation.

Practices: Design and make a purposeful Systems and Control outcome, using appropriate integration and refinement.

Design, Make, and Innovate

Develop a system for a real user need, with safe construction, a clear brief and tests that inform changes. Paraphrased knowledge and practices: p. 13; integration: p. 16.

Shared design expectations in a systems context
Common themeYear 7Year 8
Users, context and impacts

Knowledge: Outcomes affect people and places differently, with intended and unintended effects.

Practices: Evaluate existing outcomes and use a guided framework to adapt a brief to context and users.

Knowledge: Culture, environment, priorities and constraints shape design decisions.

Practices: Weigh and justify trade-offs, revising priorities as new evidence or feedback emerges.

Representing and modelling

Knowledge: Different drawing forms convey different information.

Practices: Explore ideas through sketches and drawings.

Knowledge: Visual modes represent three-dimensional ideas; functional models test ideas before final production.

Practices: Communicate form/function accurately and model design intent, accuracy and efficiency before committing resources.

Specifications and evaluation

Knowledge: Attributes describe qualities such as safety and reliability; measurable specifications enable testing.

Practices: Develop simple specifications, evaluate attributes and explain materials/technique choices.

Knowledge: Specific criteria and usability evidence support reliable evaluation.

Practices: Develop against specifications, test feasibility and investigate unintended effects.

Across both years: address ethical, cultural and social responsibilities as well as safety. AI investigation and responsible AI choice are also in the underpinning strand (p. 13); they need intentional teaching and are not implied by a motor, circuit or pre-programmed controller.

Learning Contexts

Existing kit connection - limited coverage

Minecraft Builder Kit supports planning, building and before/after comparisons. It can introduce system representation, but its current outline does not teach controlled circuits, electronic component selection or feedback loops.

A virtual structure is not evidence of real structural-force investigation. Any proposed Minecraft control simulation should be identified as a simulation, with its limitations explained and physical-system learning assessed separately.

Future activities - proposed, not yet added

  • Everyday-system investigation: safely inspect or use diagrams of a familiar system to identify interacting parts; do not dismantle powered equipment.
  • Mechanism and structure models: compare movement or load behaviour using teacher-approved materials and safe test conditions.
  • Supervised control-circuit project: use approved low-voltage educational components, with teacher-set limits and safety checks, to test input/output and control.
  • Subsystem integration challenge: combine a mechanism and approved control, document connections and refine performance from evidence.
  • Optional programming connection: add explicit coding and testing learning where students program a controller; otherwise use pre-programmed controls without claiming programming coverage.

Teacher Notes

Curriculum coverage

Map systems thinking and physical systems to p. 16, and integrated design to p. 13. Keep Digital Technology programming and data expectations distinct where connected. Record actual year level, the system investigated and the evidence of explanation, construction and testing.

Gaps to plan for

  • The existing Minecraft building outline does not establish circuit, component-selection, feedback/control or physical-force coverage.
  • Year 7 needs intentional feedback/control and accessibility work, not only naming input/output.
  • Year 8 needs evidence-based refinement and justified components, not just a working model.
  • Hardware/software/network understanding and shared impact/AI learning need explicit discussion or additional contexts.
  • Use approved educational equipment, teacher supervision and local safety procedures. No mains-voltage work or unsupervised powered-equipment disassembly is proposed.

Possible evidence - teacher judgement required

Collect standard-symbol diagrams, annotated subsystem explanations, justified component choices, mechanism/force observations, controlled test records, user feedback, revisions and evaluations against specifications. Identify simulation evidence separately from physical investigation.
