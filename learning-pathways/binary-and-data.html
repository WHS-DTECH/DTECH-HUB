Context and Rationale

Digital devices represent information in forms that computers can store and process. This theme makes an otherwise invisible process tangible through hands-on encoding and decoding activities.

Students move from a physical Binary Piano model to representations of text and images, connecting classroom tasks to familiar devices, messages and digital media.

The theme is designed as a series of manageable practical activities, not an assessment project.

Aims of Theme

  • Understand that computers represent information using binary digits (0 and 1).
  • Use practical models to encode and decode simple binary information.
  • Explore how text and images can be represented digitally, including the purpose of character encodings.
  • Recognise that choices about representing data affect how information is stored, shared and understood.

Curriculum Connections

Digital Technology

Official strand: Digital Technology (proposed Technology Years 0–10, September 2026, p. 17). School pathway: Digital Systems.

Year 8 explicitly addresses representations of text, numbers, images and Boolean values, and analysing how data is used or represented in a digital system or outcome.

Year 7 foundations include inputs, processes, outputs and data in familiar systems. The Year 7 link requires explicit teaching about how the encoded information is used in a system; binary encoding alone is not the complete Year 7 requirement.

Design, Make, and Innovate

p. 13

Applied when students design a simple encoded message or image for a purpose, represent an idea, test whether another person can decode it, and improve the representation.

Completing a conversion worksheet alone does not establish broad DMI coverage.

Contexts of Learning

Local curriculum links

  • Everyday digital communication in Hokitika: how school devices store and display text, images and messages; why readable information matters for community communication.
  • Digital access and inclusion: how messages and images can be shared accurately between devices.

Mātauranga Māori and cultural considerations

  • Use te reo Māori text, including macrons, as a purposeful example of why character encoding matters. Check spelling and meanings with reliable sources; do not imply that binary is a traditional Māori knowledge system.
  • Discuss respectful handling of information and, where appropriate, mana raraunga (Māori data sovereignty) as a reason to consider who controls and shares data. This is a discussion connection, not a claim that a worksheet teaches the full concept.

Practical Learning Sequence

  1. 1

    Binary Piano Existing

    Build or use the binary place-value model; identify 0/1 and place values.

    Resources: Existing Binary Piano template; scissors/glue if making physical model.

  2. 2

    Encoding Binary Existing

    Convert small decimal values and a short coded word using the piano.

    Resources: Existing encoding resources.

  3. 3

    Decoding Binary Existing

    Read binary values and check results with a partner.

    Resources: Existing decoding worksheet.

  4. 4

    Representing Images Existing

    Investigate a simple pixel/grid image representation; encode or recreate an image.

    Resources: Existing image representation resource.

  5. 5

    Text Compression Existing

    Explore repeated information and simple ways to shorten a representation; compare what is preserved.

    Resources: Existing text compression booklet.

  6. 6

    Parity Game Optional

    Use a card-flip/parity demonstration to detect a change in encoded information.

    Resources: Existing parity activity / cards.

  7. 7

    ASCII Planned

    Use a character table to encode/decode short text; discuss its limitations.

    Resources: Worksheet not yet written.

  8. 8

    Unicode and te reo Māori Planned

    Compare how characters including macrons can be represented; explain why wider character support matters.

    Resources: Activity not yet written.

  9. 9

    Design a coded message Optional

    Choose an audience, create a small encoded message or image, test it with a partner and refine.

    Resources: Teacher-created brief; existing models.

  10. 10

    Consolidation Optional

    Demonstrate or explain how a device uses stored data to produce an output.

    Resources: Familiar device examples; teacher discussion.

Sequence is adaptable: activities are not automatically one full lesson each. Existing activities can be offered as self-paced DTECH-HUB Activity Cards with Completed / Not Completed status. Planned activities must remain labelled as planned until created.
