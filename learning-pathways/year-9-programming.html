Teacher planning reference

Overview

Students design, develop, test and debug programs for a purpose, user and context. Year 9 uses sequence, selection, iteration, variables, input/output and reusable structures, and designs algorithms with flowcharts, pseudocode and trace tables. Year 10 adds functions, data types and simple modularity, and tests programs systematically with expected, boundary and invalid inputs.

This continues the Year 7–8 Programming & Algorithms pathway. Program quality (clear structure, readable naming, comments, testing and debugging) is named knowledge in both years.

Official Curriculum Strand

Programming is one of the four headings (Computer science, Programming, Digital media, Responsible practice) that organise the Year 9–10 Digital Technology teaching sequence. It is not a separate official strand. Source: pp. 5 and 24.

Design, Make, and Innovate underpins all Digital Technology outcomes. In both Year 9 and Year 10, each student must be taught Design, Make, and Innovate through at least two discipline-specific strands. Problem analysis and data types connect to Computer science; responsible use of code connects to Responsible practice. Source: pp. 5, 19 and 24.

Year 9 and Year 10 Knowledge and Practices

Read across each topic. Year 10 moves from working programs to maintainable, systematically tested programs. Paraphrased summaries: p. 24.

Programming: aligned Year 9 and Year 10 expectations
Common topic and pathway fitYear 9 Knowledge and PracticesYear 10 Knowledge and Practices
Programming constructs
Core programming

Knowledge: Programs use sequence, selection, iteration, variables, input/output and reusable structures to solve problems for a purpose, user and context.

Practices: Design, develop, test and debug programs using these constructs.

Knowledge: Programs are designed to be understandable, adaptable and maintainable through structure, functions and modularity.

Practices: Develop programs using variables, selection, iteration, functions, input/output, data types and simple modularity.

Algorithm design
Computer science connection

Knowledge: Algorithms are designed before and alongside code.

Practices: Design, test, debug and refine algorithms using flowcharts, pseudocode and trace tables, for tasks such as totals or averages, validating input, checking conditions, finding a value in a list or controlling a simple digital process.

Knowledge: Tracing and comparing algorithms is Year 10 Computer science knowledge.

Practices: Use trace evidence to explain changes to program logic (see Testing and debugging).

Program quality
Core programming

Knowledge: Quality is identified by clear structure, readable naming, comments, testing and debugging.

Practices: Apply clear structure, meaningful naming, comments, testing evidence and debugging.

Knowledge: Naming, comments, testing and debugging support understandability and maintenance.

Practices: Refine and explain changes to program logic or behaviour using evidence.

Testing and debugging
Core programming

Knowledge: Testing and debugging are part of program quality.

Practices: Test and debug programs and record testing evidence.

Knowledge: Testing uses expected, boundary and invalid inputs to check that a program behaves as intended.

Practices: Test with expected, boundary and invalid inputs, then use the evidence to troubleshoot and refine.

Planning note: The curriculum does not name a programming language. Choose block-based or text-based tools that let students show the named constructs; Year 10 functions and modularity are easier to evidence in text-based code.

Design, Make, and Innovate

Programs should be fit-for-purpose outcomes for real users, tested before release. Paraphrased knowledge and practices: p. 19; integration: p. 24.

Shared design expectations in a programming context
Common themeYear 9Year 10
Users and specifications

Knowledge: Effective design integrates human considerations using frameworks, stakeholder feedback and evidence.

Practices: Apply design thinking, modelling and structured processes to test and refine ideas that meet specifications.

Knowledge: Fitness for purpose relies on user information, stakeholder feedback, testing evidence and data such as UX data.

Practices: Use testing, failure analysis, stakeholder critique and performance data to justify refinements.

Responsible release

Knowledge: Responsible development requires identifying risks, disclosing known limitations and testing before release.

Practices: Develop outcomes through collaboration, stakeholder engagement and iterative improvement.

Knowledge: Design decisions balance ethical, legal and stakeholder considerations with the consequences of malfunction and failure.

Practices: Use disciplinary knowledge and collaboration to inform and justify design decisions.

AI and code

Knowledge: AI generates outputs from data, models and user inputs; output quality depends on input quality.

Practices: Test how inputs influence the accuracy and usefulness of AI outputs (for example AI-suggested code).

Knowledge: AI can bring benefits, risks, bias and unintended consequences.

Practices: Evaluate AI benefits, risks and limitations to justify responsible decisions about its use.

Across both years: manage risks and use tools and workspaces safely and purposefully, and address ethical, cultural and social responsibilities, including mana raraunga (Māori data sovereignty) where relevant (p. 19). Use non-sensitive or fictional data where possible.

Learning Contexts

Future activities - proposed, not yet added

  • Calculator or score tracker: totals and averages with input validation (named Year 9 examples).
  • Search the list: find a value in a list, traced first in a trace table.
  • Simple controller: control a simple digital process, such as a microcontroller light or a game state.
  • Test like a tester (Year 10): design expected, boundary and invalid test cases for a classmate's program and report the results.
  • MiddleDTECH Jobs: a programming-based Jobs card can give students a real client and context.

Teacher Notes

Curriculum coverage

Direct links are programming constructs, algorithm design with flowcharts, pseudocode and trace tables, program quality and testing (p. 24). Year 10 explicitly adds functions, data types, modularity and boundary/invalid testing.

Gaps to plan for

  • A working program does not on its own show testing evidence or explained debugging.
  • Year 10 boundary and invalid testing needs a deliberate test plan.
  • Copied or AI-generated code needs declared use and explanation (see Responsible practice).

Possible evidence - teacher judgement required

Collect design representations (flowcharts, pseudocode, trace tables), annotated code, test plans and results, and explanations of changes.

No grades, progress descriptors or automatic curriculum-completion rules are assigned here. Check the eventual official curriculum before treating the mapping as a compliance reference.
