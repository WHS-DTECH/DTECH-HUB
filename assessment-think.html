THINK – Build Your Ideas

Before writing your answer, use Who, What, Where, When, Why and How to explore your technical knowledge and develop your ideas. These questions help you identify relevant examples, explain how technology works and consider its effects on users.

Six things to think about

  1. 1What?

    What is the technical concept or principle?

  2. 2Who?

    Who uses it or is affected by it?

  3. 3Where?

    Where is it used or applied?

  4. 4When?

    When does it become important?

  5. 5Why?

    Why does it matter to the user?

  6. 6How?

    How does it work or affect the user?

Two types of thinking

WHAT, WHO, WHERE and WHEN

Build the facts

These questions help you identify the technical concept, people involved, context and circumstances.

WHY and HOW

Develop your explanation

These questions help you explain how technology works, why it matters and what consequences it has.

Facts tell the reader what is happening. Explanations help the reader understand how and why.

THINK in action

Before writing an answer, use the 5Ws & H to explore what you know about the topic.

WHAT?What is the technology?
Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is a security method that requires users to verify their identity using two or more different types of authentication factors.
WHO?Who uses it or is affected?
Teachers and staff accessing student information, as well as the students whose information is protected.
WHERE?Where is it used?
When signing into school accounts or systems containing confidential information.
WHEN?When is it important?
When someone signs in to a protected school account, especially when their password may have been compromised.
WHY?Why does it matter?
It reduces the risk of someone accessing private information using a stolen password.
HOW?How does it work?
A user enters their password and then verifies their identity using a different factor, such as an authenticator app or security key.

Pause and choose

Which of your 5Ws & H ideas actually help answer the examination question?

Remember

Not every idea needs to appear in your answer!

Use WHAT, WHO, WHERE and WHEN to gather relevant facts. Use WHY and HOW to develop your technical explanation.

Choose the information that directly answers the examination question. More information does not necessarily mean a better answer.
